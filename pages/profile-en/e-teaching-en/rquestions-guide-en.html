Overview

Who it is for: researchers and postgraduate students in Film and Television Studies, with priority given to qualitative and mixed-methods research.

What you get:

  • A concise research problem and research aim.
  • An initial question, then a refined main question.
  • A self-assessment on eight criteria, scored 1–5.
  • Two to four supporting questions.
  • A final report you can edit, copy, print and download.

Have ready before you start: your topic and your material or population, two or three references on what earlier studies found, and an idea of your method. If you used the Research Problem Compass first, bring its problem statement.

Moving around: Next and Previous, or the step numbers at the top. Fields marked with an asterisk (*) are required. Your answers save automatically in your browser.

Example used in this guide: a qualitative study of how young female viewers in Jeddah read the portrayal of intergenerational conflict in Saudi series on streaming platforms (2019–2025).

Accessing the tool

Open the tool from the Teaching Experience page (Digital Learning Resources) or directly at: Refining and Formulating Research Questions. Every Builder step also has a “Guidance for this step” link that opens this guide at the same step in a new tab.

Learning outcomes

After completing the tool’s steps you will be able to:

  • Move from a general topic to a concise research problem and aim.
  • Formulate a main research question suited to a qualitative, mixed-methods or quantitative approach.
  • Refine the question on eight dimensions and self-assess it on eight criteria.
  • Build coherent supporting questions that address different dimensions of the main question.

Step 2 — Clarify the research problem

One or two sentences per field are enough. Questions should follow from the problem, not the other way round.

Field What to enter Correct example
What issue or phenomenon requires investigation? * The phenomenon and its context Saudi drama on streaming platforms has expanded rapidly, and intergenerational conflict is a recurring theme.
What is currently known about it? * Key studies, with (Author, year) Studies of Gulf drama have mostly used quantitative content analysis of broadcasts (Kharroub & Weaver, 2014).
What remains insufficiently understood? * Your gap: a new context, corpus, population, method or theoretical angle How young female viewers interpret this conflict in streaming-era series.
Why is further research needed? * Why closing the gap matters and for whom To understand cultural change and to inform screenwriters.

Step 4 — Develop the initial research question

Click a ready stem (e.g. “How do audiences interpret …?”) to insert it, then complete it in your own words.

  • Qualitative: an open “how” or “what” question about one central phenomenon, with no causal or measurement language. Example: “How do young female viewers in Jeddah interpret the portrayal of intergenerational conflict in Saudi series?”
  • Mixed methods: four required fields appear: an overarching question, a quantitative question, a qualitative question, and an integration question that says how the strands combine. Integration example: “To what extent do the interviews explain the viewing patterns shown by the survey?”
  • Quantitative: name the variables, the relationship or difference, and the population.

Step 5 — Refine the research question

  1. The tool checks the question on eight dimensions: clarity of wording, specificity, scope and boundaries, alignment with the problem, theoretical relevance, methodological fit, feasibility, potential contribution.
  2. Tick the three feasibility self-check items that apply to you (access to material, timeline, skills).
  3. Use the “Suggested revision” if it fits, then write your **Refined main research question *** and watch the count of dimensions still needing attention.
Before (weak) After (better)
Do TV series affect young people’s values? How do young female viewers in Jeddah interpret the portrayal of intergenerational conflict in Saudi streaming series (2019–2025)?

Step 6 — Academic evaluation

Rate the refined question from 1 to 5 on eight criteria: clarity and precision; focus and specificity; relevance to the research problem; alignment with the research aim; theoretical coherence; methodological appropriateness; feasibility; potential academic significance. Start with “Apply suggested scores”, then adjust to your own judgement.

Step 7 — Develop supporting research questions

Write two to four questions covering different dimensions of the main question (narrative, visual style, production context, audience readings). Click a suggestion to add it and edit it, or “+ Add an empty supporting question”. Correct examples:

  1. What meanings do viewers give to the parent and child characters?
  2. How do viewers relate this conflict to their own family experiences?
  3. How do readings differ between solo and family viewing?

Step 8 — Final research question report

Generated from your answers and editable directly. Buttons: Copy report, Print / Save as PDF, Download Word (.doc), Download text (.txt), Regenerate report from my answers (this replaces your manual edits).

Reading the feedback

  • Analysis in Step 5: shows the eight dimensions with a comment on each one that needs review.
  • Counter: “Number of dimensions still needing attention” falls with each improvement.
  • Suggested revision: a rule-based template; edit it to fit your study.
  • Quality Checker (A): “Detected” means the wording rules found it; “Check manually” needs your judgement.

Saving, exporting and privacy

  • Automatic saving in this browser only; nothing is sent to a server.
  • Switching language keeps your answers (your text is not translated).
  • Another device: “Export my data (JSON)”, then “Import data”. Export a copy before clearing browser data or using private browsing.
  • Deleting: “Delete my saved data” asks for confirmation and cannot be undone without an exported copy.

Common mistakes in research questions

Mistake Weak example Instead
Yes/no question Are women portrayed stereotypically? How are working women portrayed in …?
Double-barrelled How is the family portrayed and how do audiences receive it? One main question; the other becomes a supporting question
Leading assumption Why do series distort the image of youth? How do series portray youth?
Causal language in a qualitative study What is the effect of series on values? How do viewers interpret …?
No context or boundaries How is the family represented in drama? Add the material, place and period
A search, not a research question How many Saudi series aired in 2023? A question that needs analysis and interpretation

Note: feedback and suggestions come from transparent programmed rules, not artificial intelligence, and do not replace academic supervision.

Frequently asked questions and troubleshooting

Why can’t I move to the next step?
Some required fields (marked with an asterisk) are empty or too short. A message appears at the top of the step and each missing field is highlighted with a note below it; complete them and click Next again.
Where are my answers saved?
In this browser’s local storage on this device only; nothing is sent to a server. On another device or browser your answers appear only after you import a JSON file you exported earlier.
Why did four fields appear in Step 4?
Because you chose mixed methods in Step 1; it requires an overarching, a quantitative, a qualitative and an integration question.
How many supporting questions should I write?
Two to four, each covering a different dimension of the main question without repetition.
How do I save the report as a PDF?
In the final step click the print button (“Print” or “Print / save as PDF”), then choose “Save as PDF” in your browser’s print dialog. Only the report is printed, not the rest of the site.
Are my answers translated when I switch to the Arabic version?
No. Your answers move to the other version exactly as you wrote them; only the interface and guidance change language.
My answers have disappeared. What happened?
Usually the browser data was cleared, private (incognito) browsing was used, or the tool was opened in a different browser. They can only be restored from an exported JSON file, so export a backup regularly.
Does the tool use artificial intelligence?
No. All feedback, formulations and suggested scores come from transparent programmed rules (word lists and templates), so the tool may miss a problem or flag something that is fine; your judgement and your supervisor’s come first.
