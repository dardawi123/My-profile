Overview

Introduction to the tool: the Theoretical Framework Builder is a guided laboratory for selecting, justifying, applying and writing a theoretical framework in film and television research. Each step explains a principle, shows a film or television example, collects your entries and checks them with transparent rules. All steps feed one framework draft and one report.

Academic purpose and intended users: postgraduate students, doctoral researchers and academics in the Cinema and Television track of the Department of Audio-Visual Production. The tool prioritises qualitative research (interviews, focus groups, reception studies, textual, semiotic, narrative and discourse analysis) and also supports surveys, quantitative content analysis, mixed methods and critical or theoretical work. It does not assume that every study needs one formal theory: a perspective may serve as an interpretive lens, an explanatory framework, a critical standpoint, a source of sensitising concepts or a source of hypotheses.

Main features:

  • A twelve-step Theoretical Framework Builder with required-field checks, a progress indicator and a “Guidance for this step” link in every step.
  • A Theory Explorer with eighteen profiles (feminist film theory, auteur and genre theory, Barthes’ semiology and Metz’s film semiotics, structuralism and post-structuralism, Hall’s representation theory and encoding/decoding, reception theory, uses and gratifications, cultivation, narrative theory, psychoanalytic film theory, postcolonial theory, the Frankfurt School, cultural studies, political economy and cultural proximity), with search, filters and compatibility badges.
  • A Theory Comparison Matrix showing assumptions, concepts, questions, methodological implications, strengths, limitations and documented relations (complementary, in tension, different levels).
  • A Theoretical Concepts Matrix with CSV and JSON export and import.
  • A Theory–Research Alignment Matrix linking every research question to concepts, analytical focus, evidence and procedure.
  • A Framework Visualizer: an editable map with hierarchical, relational and network layouts, draggable elements and SVG download.
  • A ten-part framework composer, a rule-based writing checker, a twelve-item refinement checklist and a ten-criterion quality evaluation.
  • A final report you can edit, copy, print, save as PDF or download as Word or text.

The workflow: Steps 1–2 define the study and what it needs from theory. Steps 3–5 explore, compare and select. Steps 6–8 define concepts and connect the theory to the questions and the method. Step 9 maps the framework. Step 10 writes it; Step 11 evaluates it; Step 12 produces the final version and report.

Have ready before you start: your problem, questions and design; the foundational texts you have actually read (with page numbers for any quotation); recent applications of the theory in film or television studies; and your institution’s or journal’s guidelines. The tool uses only what you enter. Library profiles are short orientation summaries: read the original sources before citing them.

Moving around: use Next step and Previous step, or the numbered circles. Fields marked with an asterisk (*) are required; you cannot continue until they are complete. Everything saves automatically on this device.

Example used in this guide: a qualitative semiotic study of how two Saudi streaming series construct belonging through the family home, using Hall’s representation theory supported by Barthes’ semiology.

Accessing the tool

Open the tool from the Teaching Experience page (Digital Learning Resources) or directly at: Theoretical Framework Development in Film and Television Studies. Every Builder step also has a “Guidance for this step” link that opens this guide at the same step in a new tab.

Learning outcomes

After completing the tool’s steps you will be able to:

  • Identify what the study needs from theory: interpretation, explanation, critique, sensitising concepts or hypotheses.
  • Distinguish a theory, a theoretical tradition, a critical perspective, a conceptual model and an analytical method.
  • Compare theoretical perspectives by their assumptions, concepts, methodological implications and limitations.
  • Select and justify a coherent theoretical approach, including alternatives and any combination of theories.
  • Define key concepts from original sources and connect them to the questions and methodology without predetermining findings.
  • Map the framework, write a critical theoretical framework section, self-assess it and prepare a researcher-reviewed final report.

Reading the feedback

  • Colours and statuses: “Good” (green) needs no work, “Revise” (amber) is worth a look, “Missing” (red) is an essential element that is absent, and “Note” (blue) is information to consider.
  • Compatibility badges in the Explorer: compare a profile with your focus, design and role of theory; they explain fit and do not choose for you.
  • Relations between perspectives: “complementary”, “tension” and “different levels” are documented in the library; where none appears, explain compatibility in your own words.
  • Alignment checks: flag questions without concepts, unused concepts and wording that presumes the findings.
  • Map check: warns about causal links in interpretive designs and about unconnected elements.
  • Suggested scores: appear under each criterion in Step 11 and come from the checks; accept or change them. Recommendations appear for criteria rated 3 or below.

Guidance on scholarly referencing

  • Original theorists for concepts: attribute concepts to their originators (for example Mulvey, 1975; Hall, 1997; Barthes, 1972) and give the edition you used for translations, with the original date.
  • Recent work for applications: support applications and debates with recent peer-reviewed film and television studies where possible (for example 2020 onwards).
  • Do not cite what you have not read: library profiles are orientation summaries. Read the source, or cite it as a secondary source.
  • Quotations need page numbers: the checker flags quotation marks without a page reference. Never reconstruct a quotation from memory.
  • One style, consistently: the tool assumes APA 7 (Author, Year). Check every DOI by opening it.
  • Journal rankings: do not state Q1/Q2 or indexing unless you have checked the current SJR or JCR listing.

Saving, editing, copying, printing and exporting

  • Automatic saving: every change is saved in this browser on this device; the time of the last save appears above the steps.
  • Editing: return to any step through the numbered circles. Concepts, alignment and the map are shared with their website sections.
  • Copying: use Copy next to the draft, “Copy final framework” or “Copy report”.
  • Printing and PDF: in Step 12 click “Print / Save as PDF” and choose “Save as PDF”. Only the report is printed, including the map.
  • Downloading: “Download Word (.doc)” and “Download text (.txt)”; the map can be downloaded as SVG in Step 9.
  • Concept files: export the concepts matrix as CSV (opens in Excel or Google Sheets) or JSON; import adds rows to the existing matrix.
  • Project files: “Export my project (JSON)” saves everything; “Import project” restores it on another device or browser.
  • Switching language: your entries and current step are kept; your own text is never translated.
  • Deleting: “Delete my saved data” (click twice to confirm) removes your project from this browser. Do this on shared computers.

Common mistakes

A list of theories Suggests no real framework Choose one perspective and justify any addition (Step 5)

Troubleshooting

  • “Next step” does nothing: a required field is empty or too short; the message at the top and the highlighted fields show what is missing.
  • No theories appear in the Explorer: the filters exclude them all; set Focus, Design and Analytical purpose to “All” and clear the search.
  • My secondary perspective is not listed: only shortlisted perspectives other than the main one appear. Add it to the shortlist in Step 3.
  • The draft did not change after I edited an earlier step: you edited the full draft directly. Use “Rebuild from parts” (your edits will be replaced) or edit the draft yourself.
  • A part is missing from the draft: the part is switched off, or its source is empty (for example no concept has a definition).
  • The map looks crowded: switch layout, drag elements, or delete minor elements; “Reset positions” restores the layout.
  • The CSV import failed: the first row must contain the column names exported by the tool; save the file as CSV (UTF-8).
  • Arabic text looks wrong in Excel: open the CSV through Data › From Text/CSV and choose UTF-8.
  • My data disappeared: data are stored per browser and device; private windows and clearing site data remove them. Import your exported JSON file.
  • The printed report includes other parts of the page: print from Step 12 using the button, not the browser menu.

Academic integrity and limitations

  • Your content, your responsibility: every statement in the framework comes from what you entered or from library text you chose to insert and then rewrote. You remain responsible for its accuracy and for every citation.
  • No invented content: the tool does not generate studies, findings, quotations or references. Illustrative examples are labelled and are not real studies; published examples link to verified DOIs.
  • Rule-based, not AI: checks rely on word lists, counts, citation patterns and documented theory relations. They can miss problems or flag acceptable wording, and they do not judge scholarly quality.
  • Compatibility is not a recommendation: badges and relations explain fit; the choice and its justification are yours.
  • Translation: the tool does not translate. For two languages, write each version yourself.
  • Not a substitute: the tool supports learning and refinement. It does not replace supervision, reading the original theory or peer review.

Frequently asked questions

Why can’t I move to the next step?
Some required fields (marked with an asterisk) are empty or too short. A message appears at the top of the step and each missing field is highlighted with a note below it; complete them and click Next again.
Where are my answers saved?
In this browser’s local storage on this device only; nothing is sent to a server. On another device or browser your answers appear only after you import a JSON file you exported earlier.
Does the tool choose the theory for me?
No. Badges and relations only explain fit; you choose, justify the choice and compare alternatives.
Can I cite the library profiles?
No. Profiles are short orientation summaries; read the foundational sources listed in each profile and cite them directly.
Do I have to combine two theories?
No. One coherent approach is often enough. Add a secondary perspective only if you can explain why the combination is intellectually justified.
Why does the check warn me about causal arrows?
Because interpretive research does not usually claim causal relations. Use “Relation (line)” unless your theory and design justify a directional or causal claim.
How do I move the concepts matrix into Excel?
Use “Export CSV” in Step 6 or in the Theoretical Concepts section and open the file in Excel as UTF-8. To bring it back, edit and save as CSV, then use “Import CSV or JSON”.
How do I save the report as a PDF?
In the final step click the print button (“Print” or “Print / save as PDF”), then choose “Save as PDF” in your browser’s print dialog. Only the report is printed, not the rest of the site.
Are my answers translated when I switch to the Arabic version?
No. Your answers move to the other version exactly as you wrote them; only the interface and guidance change language.
My answers have disappeared. What happened?
Usually the browser data was cleared, private (incognito) browsing was used, or the tool was opened in a different browser. They can only be restored from an exported JSON file, so export a backup regularly.
Does the tool use artificial intelligence?
No. All feedback, formulations and suggested scores come from transparent programmed rules (word lists and templates), so the tool may miss a problem or flag something that is fine; your judgement and your supervisor’s come first.
