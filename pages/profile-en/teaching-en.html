Teaching Interests

My teaching interests span drama, film, television, and media and communication studies, with particular emphasis on film and television criticism, visual media analysis, audience studies, gender representation, and comparative cultural studies. I draw on critical, theoretical, and cultural approaches to examine how audiovisual texts construct meaning and represent identity, power, and social values across different cultural contexts. In my courses, I encourage students to engage critically with screen and media texts, explore their wider cultural and ideological contexts, and develop analytical skills for interpreting diverse forms of media and cultural expression.

Interactive Research Tools

Interactive academic tools that guide postgraduate students in Film and Television Studies through the development, writing and refinement of their research projects.

  • Formulating Research Title (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them from a broad idea or working title to a clear, focused research title in film and television studies. An eight-step builder generates alternative formulations, refinement and evaluation tools check clarity, scope and terminology, and an alignment matrix connects the title to the research problem, aims, theory and methods.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Research Abstract Writing and Refinement (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them from a completed study to a concise, accurate research abstract in film and television studies. A twelve-step builder adapts to qualitative, quantitative and mixed designs, builds structured or unstructured abstracts from the researcher’s own entries, checks language, word limits and keywords, and evaluates the draft against a ten-criterion rubric.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Research Introduction Writing and Refinement (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them in constructing a coherent research introduction in film and television studies. A thirteen-step builder moves from context and a synthesised scholarly background to an evidence-supported gap or critical problem, rationale, aligned aims and questions, theoretical positioning and contribution, then assembles, refines and evaluates the introduction against a ten-criterion rubric.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Research Problem Compass (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them from an initial idea to a clear research problem in film and television studies. Students locate their research site, identify the gap, and problematise prevailing assumptions; the tool then drafts a problem statement, purpose statement, and research questions suited to qualitative or mixed-methods designs, with a twelve-criterion quality check.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Formulating Research Questions (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them from a broad research idea to a focused, researchable question in film and television studies. An eight-step builder develops the question, a quality checker and comparison tool refine its wording, and an alignment matrix connects it to the research problem, aims, theory, and methods in qualitative and mixed-methods designs.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Literature Review (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them from a review question to a critical, synthesised literature review in film and television studies. A ten-step builder covers the search strategy, screening, a literature review matrix, critical appraisal, themes and evidenced research gaps, then drafts an editable review, checks its consistency and evaluates it with a ten-criterion rubric.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Formulating Research Methodology (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them from a research problem and questions to a coherent, justified methodology in film and television studies. Students work through ten steps covering approach, paradigm, design, sampling, data collection and analysis, ethics and quality; the tool then drafts an editable methodology section, checks its consistency and evaluates it against a ten-criterion rubric.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Theoretical Framework Development (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them in selecting, justifying and writing a theoretical framework in film and television studies. A twelve-step builder moves from the study's theoretical needs through a library of eighteen perspectives, comparison, concept definition and alignment with questions and methodology to an editable framework map, a critical framework draft and a ten-criterion evaluation.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Research Findings and Discussion (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them from organised evidence to well-written findings and a critical discussion in film and television studies. Two modules move through an evidence matrix, themes, question alignment, audiovisual evidence and visualisation to interpretation, theory integration, comparison with previous studies, implications and limitations, evaluated against a fourteen-criterion rubric.

    Explore resource ↗ User Guide (opens in a new tab)

  • Research Conclusion Writing and Refinement (opens in a new tab)

    An interactive academic learning resource for postgraduate students, guiding them from the findings of a completed study to a concise, credible conclusion in film and television studies. Thirteen steps move through findings synthesis, research question alignment, contribution, implications, limitations, future research and a closing statement to a coherence checker and a twelve-criterion rubric.
