Research Conclusion Writing and Refinement

A guided laboratory for the last section of your study: return to the research problem, synthesise what the findings mean together, answer the research questions honestly, state a credible contribution and its implications, acknowledge limitations, propose specific future research, and close with a meaningful final statement.

Core tool

Research Conclusion Builder

Thirteen steps in three modules: foundations (Steps 1–2), synthesis — findings, research questions, contribution, implications, limitations, future research and closing statement (Steps 3–9) — and construction, refinement, evaluation and report (Steps 10–13). Each step explains the concept, gives a film or television example and checks your entries with transparent rules. The tool never invents findings, contributions or references. Your work is saved on this device only.

Tool A · Key Findings Synthesis Tool

Findings Synthesis

A conclusion does not repeat the results one by one: it states what they mean together. Enter the findings your study reported, mark the main finding, and write a short synthesis of their collective significance. Methodological traditions conclude differently, so the guidance adapts to your approach.

Tool B · Research Question–Conclusion Alignment Matrix

Research Question Alignment

For every research question, state how fully it was answered, give a concise answer, point to the supporting findings and add a concluding interpretation. Questions that were only partly answered — or not answered by the evidence — should be said so honestly.

Tool C · Research Contribution Builder

Scholarly Contribution

State what your study adds to film and television studies — theoretically, empirically, methodologically, contextually or critically. Every contribution should follow from a finding; avoid claims of being “the first” or “a breakthrough” unless you can show it.

Tool E · Limitations Reflection Tool

Research Limitations

Present genuine limitations transparently, explain how they affect the reading of the findings, and do not undermine the study unnecessarily. If your journal places limitations elsewhere (for example in the discussion), this part can be left out of the conclusion.

Future Research Directions

Recommendations should grow out of the completed study: unresolved questions, limitations, unexpected findings, emerging theoretical issues, contextual gaps or new methods. Say what should be studied, how and why — not simply that “more research is needed”.

      About the Tool

      About this tool

      Purpose

      This platform helps postgraduate students, doctoral researchers and academics in the Cinema and Television track of the Department of Audio-Visual Production to write and refine the conclusions of theses, journal articles and conference papers: restating the purpose, synthesising findings, answering research questions, stating contributions and implications, acknowledging limitations, recommending future research and closing the argument. It prioritises qualitative and mixed-methods research and supports quantitative and interpretive work.

      How it works

      Checks, assembled drafts and suggested scores come from transparent, rule-based JavaScript that can be read in the source files. The tool does not use artificial intelligence. Templates only arrange your own entries; it never generates findings, contributions, implications or references.

      Privacy

      Your project, including all matrices and drafts, is saved in this browser’s local storage only. Nothing is sent to a server. On a shared computer, delete your data when you finish. You can export, import or delete your data at any time.

      Academic disclaimer. This platform supports academic synthesis, conclusion writing and research refinement. It does not replace scholarly judgement, academic supervision, source verification or formal peer review, and the researcher remains responsible for every interpretation, claim and final statement.
