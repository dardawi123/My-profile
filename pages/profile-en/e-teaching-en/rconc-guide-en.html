User Guide: Research Conclusion Writing and Refinement

This tool takes you from the findings of a completed study to a concise, credible research conclusion. Steps 1–2 record the study and its purpose, Steps 3–9 synthesise the findings and build each part of the conclusion, and Steps 10–13 assemble, refine, evaluate and report it.

Overview

Introduction to the platform: the Research Conclusion Builder is an interactive synthesis and writing laboratory, not an information page. Each step explains a principle, shows a film or television example (often with weaker and stronger wording), collects your entries and checks them with transparent rules. Your entries feed one conclusion draft and one report.

Academic purpose and intended users: postgraduate students, doctoral researchers and academics in the Cinema and Television track of the Department of Audio-Visual Production who write conclusions for theses, journal articles, conference papers and book chapters on films, series, drama, audiences, representation and screen media. The tool prioritises qualitative and mixed-methods research and also supports quantitative, interpretive and critical work. It does not impose one format: qualitative conclusions emphasise interpretive insight and contextual meaning, quantitative ones the implications of observed relationships, mixed-methods ones the integrated insight, and critical work its interpretive argument.

What a conclusion does: it closes the study. It restates the purpose briefly, synthesises what the findings mean together, shows how the research questions were answered, states the contribution and implications, acknowledges limitations where appropriate, recommends specific future research and ends with a statement that gives intellectual closure. It does not repeat the results, introduce new findings or evidence, or copy the introduction.

The three modules:

  • Foundations (Steps 1–2): research profile and the restated purpose.
  • Synthesis (Steps 3–9): findings synthesis, research question alignment, contribution, implications, limitations, future research and closing statement.
  • Construction, refinement and report (Steps 10–13): the complete conclusion, the refinement assistant, the twelve-criterion evaluation and the final report.

All matrices are shared between the Builder and the website sections: what you enter in one place appears in the other. Every matrix can be exported and imported as CSV or JSON.

Have ready before you start: your research problem, aim and questions; your final findings (or results and discussion sections); the limitations you have identified; and your target journal’s or university’s requirements for the conclusion (length, headings, whether limitations belong in the discussion). The tool uses only what you enter: it never invents findings, contributions, implications or references.

Moving around: use Next step and Previous step, or the numbered circles grouped by module. Fields marked with an asterisk (*) are required; you cannot continue until they are complete. Everything saves automatically on this device.

Example used in this guide: a qualitative semiotic study of how two Saudi streaming series construct belonging through the family home, written as a journal article without subheadings.

Learning outcomes

After completing the tool’s steps you will be able to:

  • Restate the research problem and purpose concisely from the vantage point of the completed study, without copying the introduction.
  • Synthesise the principal findings into an overall meaning rather than a list, in a way that fits the methodological tradition.
  • Answer every research question concisely with supporting evidence, acknowledging partial answers.
  • Articulate a credible contribution and implications grounded in the findings, without overclaiming.
  • Present limitations in a balanced way and propose specific future research that grows out of the study.
  • Build a coherent, concise conclusion that ends with a meaningful closing statement, and self-assess it.

Step 3 — Synthesise the principal findings (Tool A)

Field Purpose Required information How to complete the field Film or TV example Common mistakes
Short label Names the finding in lists A few words — Home as moral anchor A full sentence
Finding * What your results reported One finding as already reported Copy or paraphrase your results; never add a new finding In both series, the family home is framed as the moral centre against which the city is measured. A finding that does not appear in the results
Role in the conclusion Distinguishes main from supporting Main, supporting, theme, pattern, insight or integrated Mark one main finding Main finding Marking every finding as main
Research question Feeds Step 4 MRQ, RQ1… Link each finding to the question it answers MRQ Leaving findings unlinked
Where it is reported Traceability Section, table or theme — Section 4.2; Theme 1 —
Synthesis * What the findings mean together Two to four sentences (12+ words) Start from the collective meaning (“Taken together…”), not a list Taken together, the findings show that both series construct belonging by opposing the moral certainty of the home to the anonymity of the city. “The first finding was… The second finding was…”

Summary versus synthesis: a summary repeats findings one by one; a synthesis states the overall answer the study gives. In mixed-methods studies, synthesise the integrated insight (the meta-inference) rather than each strand. Checks: no main finding, several main findings, list-like wording, statistics or quotations in the synthesis, and findings not linked to a question.

The report contains the research title and profile, research purpose, findings synthesis, research question alignment, contribution, implications, limitations, future research, closing statement, complete conclusion draft, evaluation results, your reflection, suggested improvements, the final conclusion, references and notes.

  • Meaning, not a list: ask “what do these findings say together about my problem?” and write that answer first.
  • Lead with the main finding: supporting findings, themes and patterns explain or qualify it.
  • No detail: leave quotations, statistics and scene descriptions in the results; the conclusion states their meaning.
  • By tradition: qualitative — what the themes reveal about meaning in context; quantitative — what the observed relationships imply, without repeating statistics; mixed methods — the insight that integration provides; critical — the argument the readings build.
  • Nothing new: every finding in the conclusion must already appear in the results.
  • Ground every claim: link each contribution and implication to a finding (Steps 5–6).
  • Name the conversation: say which area of film and television studies the contribution speaks to.
  • Be modest and precise: write “among the studies reviewed” rather than “the first”; prefer “suggests” to “proves”.
  • Say for whom: implications name their audience (scholars, educators, writers, platforms, policy makers).
  • Match the design: correlational findings support associations, not causes; qualitative findings support transferable insight, not statistical generalisation.
  • Composer (Step 10): each part becomes one paragraph. Reorder or switch off parts to fit the journal; headings are offered only when permitted.
  • Coherence Checker (Step 11 and Writing and Refinement): paste any conclusion or use “Use my Builder draft”. Checks are prompts for reflection; your text is never changed.
  • Paragraph map: shows the function each paragraph appears to serve from its cue words. “Unclear function” suggests a paragraph that mixes purposes or lacks a clear lead sentence.
  • Revision templates: one pair per part; replace every bracket with your own content — the checker flags placeholders left behind.
  • Colours and statuses: “Good” (green) needs no work, “Revise” (amber) is worth a look, “Missing” (red) is an essential element that is absent, and “Note” (blue) is information to consider.
  • Areas: each check names its area (for example “Research questions” or “Closing statement”) so you know which step to revisit.
  • Suggested scores: come from the checks in the related areas; accept or change them. Recommendations appear for criteria rated 3 or below.
  • Overall level: “Ready for supervisor review” (4.5+), “Strong draft”, “Developing” or “Early draft”.

Reading the feedback

  • Colours and statuses: “Good” (green) needs no work, “Revise” (amber) is worth a look, “Missing” (red) is an essential element that is absent, and “Note” (blue) is information to consider.
  • Purpose check: flags wording copied from the research profile and an overlong opening.
  • Synthesis check: distinguishes list-like wording from synthesis and flags a missing or multiple main finding.
  • Alignment check: flags questions without answers or evidence, and questions marked fully answered with no linked finding.
  • Contribution, implications, limitations and closing checks: flag originality claims and overclaiming, implications without findings, errors presented as limitations, and new evidence in the closing statement.
  • Suggested scores: appear under each criterion in Step 12 and come from the checks; accept or change them. Recommendations appear for criteria rated 3 or below.

Saving, editing, copying, printing and exporting

  • Automatic saving: every change is saved in this browser on this device; the time of the last save appears above the steps.
  • Editing: return to any step through the numbered circles. Matrices are shared with the website sections.
  • Copying: use Copy next to the draft, “Copy final conclusion” or “Copy report”.
  • Printing and PDF: in Step 13 click “Print / Save as PDF” and choose “Save as PDF”. Only the report is printed.
  • Downloading: “Download Word (.doc)”, “Download text (.txt)”, “Export all matrices (CSV)”; each matrix can also be exported separately as CSV or JSON.
  • Project files: “Export my project (JSON)” saves everything; “Import project” restores it on another device or browser.
  • Switching language: your entries and current step are kept; your own text is never translated.
  • Deleting: “Delete my saved data” (click twice to confirm) removes your project from this browser. Do this on shared computers.

Troubleshooting

  • “Next step” does nothing: a required field or matrix is incomplete; the message at the top and the highlighted fields show what is missing.
  • Step 2 already contains text: it was filled from Step 1 on your first visit; shorten and rephrase it.
  • A finding does not appear in a list: it has no statement or label yet; lists update a moment after you type.
  • The headings option is missing: your Step 1 setting is “No headings”; change it if your journal permits headings.
  • The limitations part is empty: “Limitations are addressed elsewhere” is ticked, or no complete limitation was entered.
  • The draft did not change after I edited an earlier step: you edited the draft directly. Use “Rebuild from my entries” (your edits will be replaced) or edit the draft yourself.
  • The CSV import failed: the first row must contain the column names exported by the tool; save the file as CSV (UTF-8).
  • Arabic text looks wrong in Excel: open the CSV through Data › From Text/CSV and choose UTF-8.
  • My data disappeared: data are stored per browser and device; private windows and clearing site data remove them. Import your exported JSON file.
  • The printed report includes other parts of the page: print from Step 13 using the button, not the browser menu.

Academic integrity and research ethics

  • Your content, your responsibility: every finding, answer, contribution, implication and citation comes from you. You remain responsible for its accuracy.
  • No invented content: the tool never generates findings, contributions, implications or references. Illustrative examples are labelled; published examples link to verified DOIs.
  • Honest answers: report partial answers and genuine limitations; never present an avoidable error as a limitation or claim originality you cannot show.
  • Consistency: the conclusion must agree with your results and discussion; do not introduce data, quotations or literature that were not presented earlier.
  • Rule-based, not AI: checks rely on word lists, counts, citation patterns and links between your entries. They can miss problems or flag acceptable wording and do not judge scholarly quality.
  • Not a substitute: the tool supports learning and refinement. It does not replace scholarly judgement, supervision, source verification or peer review.

Frequently asked questions

Why can’t I move to the next step?
Some required fields (marked with an asterisk) are empty or too short. A message appears at the top of the step and each missing field is highlighted with a note below it; complete them and click Next again.
Where are my answers saved?
In this browser’s local storage on this device only; nothing is sent to a server. On another device or browser your answers appear only after you import a JSON file you exported earlier.
Does the tool write my conclusion for me?
No. Templates only arrange your own entries into paragraphs and the checks are rule-based prompts; no findings, contributions, implications or references are added that you did not enter.
How is the conclusion different from the discussion?
The discussion interprets findings in detail and compares them with previous studies and theory; the conclusion closes the study: it synthesises the overall answer and states the contribution, implications, limitations and future research briefly. The Findings Synthesis section shows a table of the differences.
What do I do with a research question that was only partly answered?
Choose “Partly answered” in Step 4 and explain why in the concluding interpretation; this appears in the conclusion. It can also become a future research recommendation in Step 8.
My journal places limitations in the discussion. What should I do?
Tick “Limitations are addressed elsewhere” in Step 7; the limitations part is left out of the conclusion and is not treated as missing in the evaluation.
Should I add subheadings to the conclusion?
Only if your journal or university requires or permits them. Set this in Step 1; the headings option appears in Step 10 when headings are allowed.
How do I save the report as a PDF?
In the final step click the print button (“Print” or “Print / save as PDF”), then choose “Save as PDF” in your browser’s print dialog. Only the report is printed, not the rest of the site.
Are my answers translated when I switch to the Arabic version?
No. Your answers move to the other version exactly as you wrote them; only the interface and guidance change language.
My answers have disappeared. What happened?
Usually the browser data was cleared, private (incognito) browsing was used, or the tool was opened in a different browser. They can only be restored from an exported JSON file, so export a backup regularly.
Does the tool use artificial intelligence?
No. All feedback, formulations and suggested scores come from transparent programmed rules (word lists and templates), so the tool may miss a problem or flag something that is fine; your judgement and your supervisor’s come first.
