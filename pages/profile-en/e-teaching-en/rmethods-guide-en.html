Overview

Who it is for: postgraduate students and researchers in Film and Television Studies, with priority given to qualitative and mixed-methods research.

What you get:

  • Justified decisions on approach, paradigm, design, sampling, data collection, analysis, ethics and quality.
  • An editable draft of your methodology section, with refinement suggestions.
  • Automatic consistency checks and a Research Alignment Matrix.
  • A self-assessment on ten criteria, and a final report you can edit and download.

Have ready before you start: your working title, research problem, main and supporting questions, and objectives. If you used the Titles and Questions tools, bring their results.

Moving around: Next step and Previous step, or the numbered circles at the top. Fields marked with an asterisk (*) are required. Each step explains why it matters and gives an example. Your answers save automatically.

Example used in this guide: a qualitative reception study of how young Saudi women interpret the portrayal of family authority in Gulf dramas streamed on Shahid (2022–2024).

Accessing the tool

Open the tool from the Teaching Experience page (Digital Learning Resources) or directly at: Refining and Formulating Research Methodology. Every Builder step also has a “Guidance for this step” link that opens this guide at the same step in a new tab.

Learning outcomes

After completing the tool’s steps you will be able to:

  • Distinguish the levels of methodological decision: approach, paradigm, design, instruments and analysis.
  • Choose and justify the approach, design and sample in light of the problem and questions.
  • Specify suitable data collection, analysis, ethics and quality procedures.
  • Draft a methodology section and evaluate it against ten criteria.

Research Methodology Builder: the ten steps

Step 1 — Context and problem

Research title (working) * Your current title Family authority in streamed Gulf drama: A reception study of young Saudi women
Research context and scope * Place, platform and period Saudi Arabia; Gulf dramas streamed on Shahid, 2022–2024
Research problem * What is not yet known or understood, and why it matters We do not know how young Saudi women interpret family authority in streaming-era drama, although these series are widely watched.

Step 5 — Sampling

Field What to enter Correct example
What will you sample? * Participants, audiovisual texts, online content or documents (more than one allowed) Participants
Sampling or selection strategies * e.g. purposive, criterion, maximum variation, snowball, theoretical, probability Purposive sampling + snowball sampling
Inclusion criteria * Who or what is included Women aged 18–30 who watched at least two Gulf dramas on Shahid in 2023–2024
Exclusion criteria (optional) Who or what is excluded Media professionals
Proposed sample size or case scope * An approximate, justified number 12–16 interviews
Recruitment or selection procedure * How you will reach the sample Invitations through university mailing lists, followed by referrals
Feasibility and access (optional) Possible obstacles Interviews in Arabic, online where needed
Why are these participants relevant? * The link between sample and questions They are the main audience of these series and live through the changes in family authority.

Ethical considerations * Tick what applies; items recommended for your design are starred Informed consent, confidentiality and anonymisation, secure storage, formal ethics approval
Quality and rigour procedures * Procedures that fit your approach (reflexivity, thick description, triangulation, inter-coder reliability…) Researcher reflexivity, thick description, audit trail

Step 9 — Draft and refine

  1. Write the limitations and methodological justifications: what your design cannot do, and why it is still the most appropriate. Example: “Purposive sampling does not allow statistical generalisation, but the aim is to understand meanings, not measure their prevalence.”
  2. The tool drafts your methodology section from your answers; you can edit it directly. “Rebuild draft” replaces your edits after a confirmation click.
  3. Read the refinement suggestions under the draft and complete any square brackets [ ].
↑ Back to contents

Step 10 — Evaluate and report

  • Review the consistency checks: fix “Fix” items first, then “Check” items.
  • Rate the methodology from 1 to 5 on ten criteria (alignment with the problem and questions, approach, design justification, sampling adequacy, data collection, analytical coherence, ethics, rigour, feasibility and transparency). Start with “Apply suggested scores”.
  • The final report is editable: copy, print, download Word (.doc) or text (.txt).

Reading the feedback

  • Approach suggestion: in Step 2 the tool suggests an approach from the wording of your questions; the decision is yours.
  • Unsuitable designs: hidden or marked in Step 4 according to the approach you chose.
  • Recommended items: starred in Step 8, with warnings about mismatches.
  • Consistency checks: “Fix”, then “Check”, then “Consistent”; start with what needs fixing.

Common methodological mistakes

Mistake Example Instead
Approach does not fit the question A “how do … interpret?” question with a quantitative survey A qualitative approach with interviews
Mixing levels “A descriptive analytical method” used as design and instrument at once Separate the approach, design, collection method and analysis
Unjustified sample size “Interviews with 20 participants” with no reason Justify adequacy through saturation or information power
Analysis not described “Data will be analysed qualitatively” Name the method and its stages
Quantitative quality criteria in a qualitative study “Validity and reliability” for interviews Trustworthiness: reflexivity, thick description, audit trail
Ignoring material rights Publishing stills from series without consideration State your fair use policy or permissions

Note: the draft, checks and scores come from transparent programmed rules, not artificial intelligence. They do not replace academic supervision or formal ethics approval.

Frequently asked questions and troubleshooting

Why can’t I move to the next step?
Some required fields (marked with an asterisk) are empty or too short. A message appears at the top of the step and each missing field is highlighted with a note below it; complete them and click Next again.
Where are my answers saved?
In this browser’s local storage on this device only; nothing is sent to a server. On another device or browser your answers appear only after you import a JSON file you exported earlier.
Why can’t I see the design I want in Step 4?
It does not fit the approach you chose in Step 2. Go back to Step 2 if the approach needs reviewing.
Will my edits to the draft be lost?
No, unless you click “Rebuild draft” and confirm with a second click; the draft is then rebuilt from your answers.
How do I save the report as a PDF?
In the final step click the print button (“Print” or “Print / save as PDF”), then choose “Save as PDF” in your browser’s print dialog. Only the report is printed, not the rest of the site.
Are my answers translated when I switch to the Arabic version?
No. Your answers move to the other version exactly as you wrote them; only the interface and guidance change language.
My answers have disappeared. What happened?
Usually the browser data was cleared, private (incognito) browsing was used, or the tool was opened in a different browser. They can only be restored from an exported JSON file, so export a backup regularly.
Does the tool use artificial intelligence?
No. All feedback, formulations and suggested scores come from transparent programmed rules (word lists and templates), so the tool may miss a problem or flag something that is fine; your judgement and your supervisor’s come first.
