Methodological guide

Formulating research questions in film and television studies

Evidence-based recommendations are labelled and cited. Illustrative examples were written for teaching and are labelled as such.

Evidence-based recommendation

Characteristics of a strong research question

  • Clear and precise: key concepts are defined; no vague terms (Mattick et al., 2018).
  • Focused: one central issue or phenomenon, with stated boundaries (Creswell & Poth, 2018).
  • Researchable: answerable with available evidence, not by looking up a single fact or by opinion (attached video tutorials).
  • Feasible, interesting, novel, ethical and relevant — the FINER criteria (Hulley et al., 2013).
  • Aligned with goals, conceptual framework, methods and validity strategies (Maxwell, 2013).
  • Significant: challenges or extends existing assumptions rather than only filling a gap (Alvesson & Sandberg, 2011, 2024).
Evidence-based recommendation

Qualitative and mixed methods questions

  • Qualitative studies usually have one central question and a few sub-questions, worded with “how” or “what” (Creswell & Poth, 2018).
  • Developing qualitative questions is a reflective, iterative process; questions may be refined during fieldwork (Agee, 2009; Lim, 2025).
  • Mixed methods studies combine quantitative, qualitative and integration questions; the design should show how the strands are integrated (Tashakkori & Creswell, 2007; Fetters et al., 2013).
  • The type of question determines the analysis procedures (Onwuegbuzie & Leech, 2006).
Evidence-based recommendation

Aligning questions with theory

  • Distinguish theory, theoretical framework and conceptual framework; the conceptual framework justifies why the question matters and how the study is designed (Varpio et al., 2020).
  • Theory shapes almost every part of a qualitative study, but over-reliance can hide emergent findings (Collins & Stockton, 2018).
  • A review of Arabic communication research found that many studies apply established theories without developing their assumptions in digital contexts (Abdelhamid, 2022) — a reminder to make the theoretical contribution explicit.

Common weaknesses to avoid

  • Overly broad questions (“How is women’s image in Arab media?”).
  • Ambiguous concepts (“impact”, “role”, “media”, “society”).
  • Double-barrelled questions asking two things at once.
  • Yes/no questions that require no analysis.
  • Leading assumptions (“How do films distort…?”) and opinion-based wording (“best”, “should”).
  • Wording that the proposed method cannot answer (e.g., “to what extent” in a purely qualitative study).
Check your question

Narrowing or broadening a question

  • Narrow by specifying who (population), what (aspect), where (context, platform) and when (period) — attached video tutorials.
  • Broaden when too little material or literature exists, by relaxing one boundary at a time.
  • Rather than accepting a ready-made topic, actively construct the phenomenon you study (Alvesson & Sandberg, 2024).
Evidence-based recommendation

Feasibility and sample size

  • A systematic review of empirical tests found saturation within 9–17 interviews or 4–8 focus groups, mainly for relatively homogeneous groups and narrowly defined objectives; broader questions need larger samples (Hennink & Kaiser, 2022).
  • Rigour depends on alignment between question, design, sampling and analysis (Johnson et al., 2020; Busetto et al., 2020).
  • Arabic media scholars note practical challenges in qualitative new-media research, such as defining categories and documenting sources (El-Dagher, 2022).

About the tool

About this tool

Purpose

This platform helps researchers and postgraduate students in film and television studies, in the Department of Audio-Visual Production, to develop, evaluate and refine academically rigorous research questions. It prioritises qualitative and mixed methods research.

How it works

All feedback is produced by transparent, rule-based JavaScript (word lists and patterns that can be inspected in the source files) and editable templates. The tool does not use artificial intelligence, does not verify your claims against the literature, and does not search databases.

Privacy

Your answers are saved only in this browser’s local storage, so you can return later. Nothing is sent to a server. You can export, import or delete your data at any time.

Academic disclaimer. This platform provides methodological guidance and educational assistance. It does not replace scholarly judgement, academic supervision or the verification of research evidence. Self-assessment scores are reflective aids, not validated measures of quality.
