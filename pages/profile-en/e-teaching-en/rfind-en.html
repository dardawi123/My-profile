Core tool

Research Findings and Discussion Builder

Module A (Steps 1–8) organises evidence and builds the findings section; Module B (Steps 9–17) builds the discussion; Steps 18–19 evaluate and report. Each step explains the concept, gives a film or television example and checks your entries with transparent rules. The tool never invents findings, quotations, statistics or references. Your work is saved on this device only.

      Methodological and applied sources prioritise 2020–2026; foundational reporting standards and methodological works published earlier are included where they remain the standard references. Journal quartiles (Q1/Q2) and indexing are not stated because they change yearly; check the current SJR or JCR listing before citing a ranking.

      About the Tool

      About this tool

      Purpose

      This platform helps postgraduate students, doctoral researchers and academics in the Cinema and Television track of the Department of Audio-Visual Production to organise evidence, present findings, interpret results, integrate theory, compare findings with previous studies and write rigorous discussions for theses, articles and conference papers. It prioritises qualitative and mixed-methods research and supports quantitative and interpretive work.

      How it works

      Checks, suggested structures, drafts and suggested scores come from transparent, rule-based JavaScript that can be read in the source files. The tool does not use artificial intelligence. Templates only arrange your own entries; it never generates findings, quotations, statistics, interpretations or references.

      Privacy

      Your project, including all matrices, charts and drafts, is saved in this browser’s local storage only. Nothing is sent to a server. On a shared computer, delete your data when you finish. You can export, import or delete your data at any time.

      Academic disclaimer. This platform provides methodological guidance for organising, writing, interpreting and discussing research findings. It does not replace expert scholarly judgement, academic supervision or formal peer review, and the researcher remains responsible for every interpretation, claim and citation.
